<h2 id="how-i-prepared">How I prepared</h2>

I bought three resources:

- **[Udemy course](https://www.udemy.com/course/aws-data-engineer/)** — Frank Kane and Stéphane Maarek.
- **[Udemy practice exams](https://www.udemy.com/course/practice-exams-aws-certified-data-engineer-associate-r/)** — Stéphane Maarek and Abhishek Singh.
- **[Tutorials Dojo DEA-C01 practice exams](https://portal.tutorialsdojo.com/courses/aws-certified-data-engineer-associate-practice-exam-dea-c01/)** — Jon Bonso.

I watched the videos at **2× speed for familiar services** and **1–1.5× for unfamiliar ones**.
I took two practice exams straight through, reviewing them afterward, and two in practice mode,
studying each question individually. I found using pencil and paper to take notes helped me 
remember details better. +1 for analog 👍🏻

The practice exams helped me learn the wording and logic of AWS questions. Even with years of production
experience, the style can trip you up. My reading workflow was:

1. **Read the final question first.**
    - What is the goal?
2. **Find the qualifier.** 
    - Which constraint matters most?
    - This usually says something like "most cost-effective," "least operational overhead," or "best performance."
3. **Read the scenario.** 
    - What services are being used?
    - Where is data coming from and going to? 
    - What are the access patterns?
4. **Eliminate answers**
    - Exclude answers that don't meet the goal/scenario?
    - Choose managed services over self-managed services.
    - Try to identify the 2 wrong, 1 overengineered pattern. 

<h2 id="what-surprised-me">What surprised me about the exam</h2>

The exam consists of 65 questions and lasts 130 min. I practiced my reading workflow on the practice exams, 
so that I could consistently answer a question in 2 min. It worked out, but I made it to the end with 
2 min to spare. The two hours of constant, critical focus was one of the most challenging aspects of 
the whole exam.

There were advanced SQL scenarios I hadn't encountered in years of production work, which made
me wonder how closely my experience matched the intended audience. Iceberg and Glue native integration like
automatic compaction were mentioned a few times. I felt the courses didn't cover them at the depth I encountered 
on the exam. Luckily, I had experience with Iceberg and was able to reason through the questions.

My work has largely been serverless, so managing EMR, OpenSearch, or Redshift clusters is less
familiar territory. I'm unsure how much I'll use that knowledge compared with their serverless
counterparts. The exam's coverage and my day-to-day work didn't line up everywhere.

<h2 id="what-carries-into-work">Reading the architecture like an exam question</h2>

Here's how I'd apply the exam question-reading workflow to the diagram above.

### Question and qualifiers

Which serverless solution meets the requirements with the **least operational overhead** and
**lowest compute and storage costs**?

### Scenario

- Aggregate source data from an API Gateway into a daily report for a website dashboard.
- The aggregation takes only a couple of seconds.
- Query historical trends for quarterly growth reports using a data catalog
- Archive reports after a year.
